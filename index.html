Abstract

Pathologists cut serial sections for extra stains and deeper levels, yet each costly section stays unknown until it is cut and stained. A world model that forecasts, from the current section, how the next one changes could preview it before cutting. However, registered serial sections are scarce, which limits the training of such a model. Directly training an image generator on these few pairs raises two problems: (a) Adjacent sections share most of their structure, so a forecast that predicts no change still scores high on image similarity. (b) Designed for realism, the generators we test predict changes that are too large and off the true direction. Their unscaled single samples thus lie farther from the true section in feature space than the current section does. To address these problems, we propose HistoDelta, a world model that forecasts the change between sections in frozen pathology features. To make up for the scarce pairs, HistoDelta is first pretrained on 3D prostate volumes and then fine-tuned on the target dataset. For (a), we score each forecast by how much of the error of reusing the current section it removes. For (b), HistoDelta keeps the tokens of the current section and adds a learned change to each, initialized at zero. Trained on only 1% of the skin patients, HistoDelta has the lowest average error among the forecasters we trained on the same data. On held-out prostate biopsy volumes, it also beats reusing the current section at deeper intervals of 100–300 µm. Our code will be made publicly available.

Limitations & FAQ

Does HistoDelta generate the next section?

No. It forecasts CONCH feature tokens (14×14 per 248 µm patch), not images, and only for single patches, not whole slides. Rendering forecasts as images and extending them to whole slides are future work.

What are the skin pairs?

Registered serial H&E sections of skin basal-cell carcinoma from COBRA. The archive does not record the spacing between sections, and most serial groups hold only two sections (1,936 of 1,967). Each pair is scored once, forecasting the warped section from the fixed one.

Where does HistoDelta not win?
  • With all training patients, the direct transformer scores higher (0.319 vs 0.305; Table 2, Test column).
  • From 5% of the training patients, the rescaled direct transformer overtakes HistoDelta (Figure 5; Supp. Table S5).
  • On UNI2-h features, ridge regression and DINO-Foresight lead (Supp. Table S7: 0.322 and 0.297 vs 0.289).
  • At 200 and 300 µm, ridge regression localizes changes within a pair better (Supp. Table S8).
How were tumor labels obtained?

Automatically, from a linear probe on H‑optimus‑1 features; the serial sections carry no tumor outlines. A pathologist blind to the labels agreed with them in 34 of 40 pairs.

Did pathologists confirm the change maps?

Not yet. In one reading, the pathologist rated every outlined region of both maps as only partly changed. In a second, blinded reading by the same pathologist on 48 pairs, the outlines of HistoDelta and a fine-tuned generator were about the same in 40 pairs, and HistoDelta was chosen in the other 8. The reader said this localization task could not be judged by eye, so we do not treat it as evidence. A slide-level reader study is future work.

What data has it been tested on?

Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded skin sections (COBRA) and six prostate biopsy volumes. Frozen sections and other 3D datasets with recorded spacing are untested.

Is feature-space accuracy clinically useful?

Not shown. The paper scores how much of copy's feature error a forecast removes; clinical utility has not been tested.
